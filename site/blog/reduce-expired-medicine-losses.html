How to cut expired-medicine losses in your pharmacy

By Syed Hashir Ali, Founder of Nuskha · 12 Nov 2026

Every strip that expires on the shelf is cash you paid and will never get back. Most pharmacies don't lose it through one big mistake but through many small ones. These five habits close the gaps.

1. Sell the earliest expiry first — every time

When two batches of the same medicine are on the shelf, the newer one usually gets sold because it's at the front. Arrange shelves so older batches face out, and use software that picks the earliest-expiry batch on the bill automatically.

2. Look 90 days ahead, not at today

By the time a medicine expires it's too late. A weekly look at everything expiring in the next 30, 60 and 90 days gives you time to move it: put it at the counter, offer it to regular customers, or return it.

3. Agree returns with your suppliers in writing

Many distributors take back near-expiry stock within a window. Know each supplier's rule, and keep the batch numbers from your purchase bills so a claim is easy.

4. Buy what actually sells

Over-buying slow movers is the biggest source of expiry. Check what sold in the last few months and set reorder levels per medicine instead of buying by habit.

5. Count stock regularly

A monthly count of high-value medicines catches mistakes early — wrong batch on the shelf, items never received, or quiet theft.
